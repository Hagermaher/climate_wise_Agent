Causes of climate change
Climate change today is driven mainly by greenhouse gas emissions from human activity. Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, and gas) for electricity, transport, and industry is the largest source. Deforestation, industrial agriculture, and waste also add gases to the atmosphere. Carbon dioxide (CO2) and methane (CH4) are the main gases that trap heat.

How the greenhouse effect works
Greenhouse gases let sunlight reach the Earth but slow down the heat that escapes back to space. This effect is natural and keeps the planet warm enough for life. Human activity makes it stronger, so the planet warms.

Global warming so far
According to the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report, global surface temperature in 2011-2020 was about 1.1 degrees C higher than in 1850-1900. The CO2 level in the atmosphere is now well above 400 parts per million, compared with about 280 parts per million before the industrial era.

Sea level rise
Sea level rises because ice sheets and glaciers melt and because ocean water expands when it gets warmer. The IPCC reports that global mean sea level rose by about 20 cm between 1901 and 2018. Low-lying coastal areas and river deltas are very exposed. The Nile Delta in Egypt is one of them, with risks of flooding, land loss, and salt water moving into farmland and groundwater.

Heat and extreme weather
Warming makes heatwaves more frequent and more intense. It also changes rainfall: some regions get stronger floods and storms, while others get longer droughts. Cities can be even hotter than the countryside because of concrete and less greenery.

Water and agriculture
Higher temperatures increase evaporation and water demand. Heat and water shortage can reduce crop yields, especially for wheat, rice, and maize, which puts food security at risk. Countries that depend on one main water source, such as Egypt with the Nile, are sensitive to changes in water supply.

Health
Heat stress, air pollution, and the spread of diseases carried by insects (for example mosquitoes) can increase as temperatures rise. Children, older people, and outdoor workers are more at risk during heatwaves.

Ecosystems
Warmer oceans cause coral bleaching. Many land and sea species lose their habitat or have to move to cooler places. Changes can disturb food chains and fisheries.

Solutions: reducing emissions (mitigation)
Shifting to renewable energy such as solar and wind, improving energy efficiency, protecting forests, using public transport, and reducing waste all lower emissions. The Paris Agreement aims to keep global warming well below 2 degrees C and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 degrees C.

Solutions: adapting to the changes (adaptation)
Adaptation means preparing for effects that are already happening, for example protecting coasts, saving water, using heat-resistant crops, building early warning systems, and planting trees in cities.

Weather versus climate
Weather is what happens on a given day or week. Climate is the average weather over a long period, usually 30 years or more. One hot year does not prove climate change by itself, and a short data record of about 10 years can show variability but cannot show a long-term trend with confidence.
